Aromatic Grass

Lemongrass

Cymbopogon citratus (DC.) Stapf

About this plant

Lemongrass is a tall, clump-forming grass known for its strong lemon scent. It is grown in home gardens and on farms across South and Southeast Asia, and is used in cooking, herbal teas and traditional plant practices.

How to identify it

  • A perennial grass growing in dense tufts, usually 1 to 1.5 m tall.
  • Long, narrow, blade-like leaves with rough, sharp edges.
  • A pale, slightly swollen base made of tightly overlapping leaf sheaths.
  • Crushing a leaf gives a strong lemon smell, the easiest way to confirm it.
  • It rarely flowers when grown in gardens.

Traditional uses and importance

  • The leaves and stem bases flavour curries, soups and teas in Sri Lankan cooking.
  • Lemongrass tea is a common household drink.
  • It has a place in traditional practices for colds and indigestion.
  • The essential oil is used in soaps, perfumes and mosquito repellents.

Garden notes

Lemongrass grows best in full sun and well-drained soil, and tolerates dry spells once established. It is propagated by dividing clumps or planting rooted stalks, and harvested by cutting stalks near the base. It makes a useful border plant and helps teach visitors about grass structure and aromatic plants.

Safety note

Lemongrass is safe as a normal food flavouring. The concentrated essential oil should not be swallowed and can irritate skin if used undiluted. Anyone who is pregnant or on medication should consult a health professional before using it medicinally.
