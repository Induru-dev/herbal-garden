Butterfly Pea

Clitoria ternatea L.
නිල් කටරොළු
  • Local nameNil Katarolu (නිල් කටරොළු)
  • ScientificClitoria ternatea L.
  • FamilyFabaceae (Pea family)
  • Plant habitPerennial twining vine

About this plant

Nil Katarolu (butterfly pea or blue pea) is a twining vine in the pea family, native to tropical Asia and widely grown in Sri Lanka. It is loved for its deep blue flowers and grows easily along fences and other supports.

How to identify it

  • A climbing or twining vine with slender, green, branched stems.
  • Compound leaves arranged alternately, usually with 5 to 7 oval leaflets.
  • Leaflets have smooth edges and rounded or slightly pointed tips.
  • Large deep blue or violet flowers with a yellowish-white centre.
  • Long, narrow, flat pods containing several seeds.
  • Roots may have small nodules that hold nitrogen-fixing bacteria.

Traditional uses and importance

  • Flowers are used to make naturally blue herbal drinks and teas.
  • Flower extracts are used as a natural food colouring.
  • The flowers are used in traditional herbal preparations.
  • Grown as an ornamental climber in home gardens.
  • As a legume, it can improve soil fertility through nitrogen fixation.

Flowers and fruits

The flowers are shaped a little like butterflies. They are usually deep blue with a yellowish-white centre, though white-flowered plants also exist. Each flower has both male and female parts, and after pollination it forms a flat pod that turns brown and splits open to release its seeds. The blue colour comes from natural pigments called anthocyanins.

Garden notes

Nil Katarolu grows well in sunny spots with moderate watering and well-drained soil, and tolerates many soil types. It is usually grown from seed. It is useful for teaching about compound leaves, flower structure, pods and nitrogen fixation.

Safety note

Use only correctly identified plants and food-grade flowers in drinks. Avoid concentrated extracts or home remedies without professional advice.
