Sandalwood

Santalum album L.

  • Local nameSudu Handun (සුදු හඳුන්)
  • ScientificSantalum album L.
  • FamilySantalaceae
  • Plant habitMedium-sized, semi-parasitic evergreen tree

About this plant

White sandalwood is the best known member of the genus Santalum, a group of about 25 species found across Southeast Asia, Australia and the islands of the South Pacific. It is semi-parasitic: it makes its own food but also draws water and nutrients from the roots of nearby plants. It is famous for its fragrant heartwood.

How to identify it

  • A medium-sized evergreen tree with smooth, yellowish-brown bark.
  • Green, shiny leaves in pairs, oval to lance-shaped, with smooth margins.
  • Small reddish-purple flowers produced in clusters.
  • Pale yellow heartwood that is famous for its aroma.
  • Its roots attach to host plants such as grasses, shrubs or other trees through special connections called haustoria.

Traditional uses and importance

  • Valued for centuries for its aroma and its place in many cultures, especially in Ayurvedic medicine.
  • Traditionally used in supportive treatments for urinary, digestive and skin complaints.
  • Sandalwood oil is traditionally believed to help with stress relief and inflammation, and is being studied for its antimicrobial and antioxidant properties.
  • Widely used in perfumes, incense and religious practices.

Garden notes

A valuable medicinal, aromatic and economically important plant. The most useful parts are the heartwood and its oil, which have a strong, pleasant and characteristic fragrance.

Safety note

Traditional uses do not prove that a plant can cure diseases. Seek qualified advice before using sandalwood oil medicinally.
