Turmeric

About this plant

Turmeric is a perennial herbaceous plant widely cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions. It is well known for its underground rhizomes, which have a bright yellow-orange colour and a distinctive aroma. It is commonly grown as a spice crop and is an important ingredient in traditional food.

How to identify it

  • Long, broad, green leaves arranged around a short pseudostem.
  • Grows from underground rhizomes that are yellow to orange inside.
  • Produces pale yellowish flowers with green and pinkish bracts.
  • The bright underground rhizomes are its most distinctive feature.

Traditional uses and importance

  • The rhizomes are widely used as a spice and a natural food colouring.
  • Commonly used in curries, sauces and other traditional dishes.
  • Used in traditional cultural practices and some traditional plant preparations.
  • Its yellow colour comes mainly from a natural pigment called curcumin.

Garden notes

Turmeric belongs to the same family as ginger. It grows well in warm, humid conditions with partial shade and well-drained, fertile soil, and it is propagated mainly through pieces of rhizome. It is an important crop because of its culinary, cultural and economic value.

Safety note

Turmeric is commonly used in food in normal culinary quantities. Concentrated turmeric products and supplements may have different safety considerations, so follow appropriate guidance when using them.
