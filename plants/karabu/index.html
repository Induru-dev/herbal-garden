Clove

About this plant

Clove is a tropical evergreen tree whose small, reddish-brown dried flower buds are used as a spice. Cloves were among the most important goods of the early spice trade and are believed to be native to the Moluccas (the Spice Islands) of Indonesia. Because harvest seasons differ between countries, fresh cloves are available throughout the year.

How to identify it

  • A medium-sized evergreen tree, about 10 to 20 m tall.
  • The bark is smooth and grey.
  • Leaves are simple, opposite, shiny, leathery and lance-shaped.
  • Leaves have small oil glands and a strong aroma when crushed.
  • Flowers grow in clusters at the ends of branches.
  • Flower buds turn pinkish-red or bright red as they mature.
  • The dried bud has a characteristic nail-like shape.

Traditional uses and importance

  • Clove is rich in natural compounds with antioxidant activity.
  • Clove oil is traditionally used for burns and wounds, and as a pain reliever in dental care, including toothache.
  • Traditionally used to help reduce gas and indigestion.
  • Widely used in perfumes and soaps, and in laboratory work as a clearing agent for tissue samples.

Garden notes

A valuable medicinal, spice and economically important plant. The unopened flower buds are harvested and dried to produce cloves. The whole plant, especially the leaves and buds, has a strong aroma due to its essential oils.
