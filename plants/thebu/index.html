Thebu

About this plant

Thebu is a tropical perennial herb native to Sri Lanka. It has upright, fleshy stems with green leaves arranged in a spiral, and attractive white flowers with a yellow centre surrounded by red bracts. It grows from underground rhizomes and is valued as a medicinal and food plant.

How to identify it

  • An erect herb with a strong, leafy stem.
  • Simple, long green leaves arranged in a spiral around the stem.
  • Dense flower spikes at the stem tips with bright red bracts.
  • Large white flowers with a yellow centre.
  • Reddish, three-angled seed capsules.

Traditional uses and importance

  • The leaves are eaten as a vegetable in Sri Lanka.
  • Leaves, roots and rhizomes are recorded in traditional Ayurvedic practices.
  • Traditionally associated with blood sugar management.

Garden notes

Thebu prefers moist, shady places. It can be propagated through underground rhizomes and seeds, and is valued in home gardens for its traditional importance and attractive flowers. It is classified as Least Concern (LC) in the national conservation assessment.

Good to know

You may also see this plant listed under its older name Costus speciosus.

Safety note

These uses represent traditional knowledge and do not prove that the plant can treat or prevent diseases.
