Prickly Chaff Flower

Achyranthes aspera L.
කරල් හැබ
  • Local nameKaral Heba (කරල් හැබ)
  • ScientificAchyranthes aspera L.
  • FamilyAmaranthaceae (Amaranth family)
  • Plant habitErect branching perennial herb

About this plant

Karal Heba (known as Nayuruvi in Tamil) is a common herb of roadsides, fields and open land in Sri Lanka. It is easy to recognise by its long flower spikes, whose prickly seeds hook onto clothes and animal fur.

How to identify it

  • Flower spikes (most distinctive): long, slender, upright spikes, up to 30 to 60 cm long, at the stem tips. The tiny greenish-white or purplish flowers and seeds point sharply downward and have stiff, prickly bracts that hook onto clothing.
  • Leaves: opposite pairs, oval to broadly elliptic, 3 to 10 cm long, with a slightly wavy edge and a soft, velvety feel.
  • Stems: an erect, branching herb 0.5 to 1.5 m tall, with somewhat four-angled, stiff, hairy stems and swollen nodes.

Traditional uses and importance

  • Coughs and fever: decoctions have traditionally been used for coughs, asthma symptoms and fevers.
  • Skin and wounds: leaf pastes are applied externally for swelling and skin irritations, and the ash of the burnt plant has been used on wounds.
  • Digestion and urine: root decoctions have been used as diuretics and for digestive complaints and piles.
  • Teeth: stems or stem ash are used in rural traditions for cleaning teeth and for toothache.
  • Household: the potash-rich ash has traditionally been used as a cleaning agent, including for washing clothes.
  • Sandalwood growing: research in Sri Lanka has identified it as a host plant for sandalwood (Santalum album), which feeds partly from the roots of nearby plants.

Garden notes

Karal Heba thrives in full sun to partial shade and grows naturally along roadsides and garden borders in the low and mid country. It tolerates poor, sandy soils as long as drainage is good and needs little water once established. It grows easily from ripe seeds. Because its sticky seeds spread readily on pets and clothing, grow it in a dedicated corner or container and trim the flower spikes before they dry.

Safety note

Folk remedies, including those for bites and stings, are not a substitute for medical care. Always seek medical help for snake bites and other emergencies.
