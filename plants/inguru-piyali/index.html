Inguru Piyali

Kaempferia galanga L.
ඉඟුරු පියලි

  • ScientificKaempferia galanga L.
  • FamilyZingiberaceae (Ginger family)
  • Plant habitLow-growing perennial herb

About this plant

Inguru Piyali (aromatic ginger) is a small, low-growing, aromatic herb in the ginger family. Its underground rhizomes have a pleasant, spicy smell. It is native to parts of Asia and is grown in Sri Lanka as a medicinal and ornamental plant.

How to identify it

  • Grows close to the ground, with very short or almost no stems.
  • Usually two or three broad, oval green leaves from the base.
  • Leaves spread out flat, close to the soil.
  • Fleshy, aromatic rhizomes that are light brown outside.
  • The whole plant has a characteristic spicy smell.

Traditional uses and importance

  • The rhizome is commonly used in traditional herbal preparations.
  • Used as an aromatic spice in traditional food.
  • Different parts of the plant are recorded in traditional medicinal practices.

Flowers and fruits

Small, attractive flowers, usually white with purple markings, appear close to the base of the leaves. They are fragrant and delicate and usually last only a short time.

Garden notes

Inguru Piyali grows well in warm, tropical climates. It prefers partial shade and moist, fertile soil, and can be propagated using its rhizomes. It is well suited to home and herbal gardens.

Safety note

Traditional uses do not prove that the plant is an effective or safe treatment. Correct identification and professional advice are important before any medicinal use.
