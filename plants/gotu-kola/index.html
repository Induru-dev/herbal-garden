Gotu Kola

Centella asiatica (L.) Urb.
ගොටුකොළ
  • Local nameGotu Kola (ගොටුකොළ)
  • ScientificCentella asiatica (L.) Urb.
  • FamilyApiaceae
  • Plant habitSmall creeping perennial herb

About this plant

Gotu Kola is a small creeping herb with round leaves, eaten almost daily in many Sri Lankan homes as a salad (mallung), porridge (kola kenda) or sambol.

How to identify it

  • A small creeping herb that spreads along the ground.
  • Round to kidney-shaped leaves, like a fan, with scalloped edges.
  • Long, creeping stems, with roots growing from the nodes.
  • Very small pinkish-white flowers in clusters.
  • Leaves have a mild scent when crushed.

Traditional uses and importance

  • Food: used as kola mallum, kola kenda and sambol, and rich in vitamins and iron.
  • Memory: traditionally believed to improve memory and concentration and to reduce mental stress, and given to children as kola kenda.
  • Wounds: crushed leaves are traditionally applied to cuts and wounds.
  • Blood: traditionally believed to purify the blood and help with blood pressure.
  • Skin: traditionally used to keep skin healthy.

Garden notes

Gotu Kola likes cool, shady and moist places and needs regular watering. It does not grow well in dry, sandy soil and will die if left to dry out in hot weather. It can be grown in a pot or in the ground, from seeds or stem cuttings.

Safety note

These are traditional beliefs, not proven medical treatments. Anyone on medication for blood pressure or other conditions should talk to their doctor.
