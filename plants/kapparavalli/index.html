Aromatic Medicinal Herb

Kapparavalli

Plectranthus amboinicus (Lour.) Spreng.
කප්පරවල්ලිය
  • Local nameKapparavalli, Karpooravalli (කප්පරවල්ලිය)
  • ScientificPlectranthus amboinicus (Lour.) Spreng.
  • FamilyLamiaceae (Mint family)
  • Plant habitFleshy perennial herb

About this plant

Kapparavalli (Indian borage) is a small, fleshy perennial herb native to southern and eastern Africa and now widely grown in Sri Lanka and South India. It is valued in Sri Lankan traditional knowledge, especially for coughs and colds.

How to identify it

  • Usually grows about 30 to 50 cm tall.
  • Thick, fleshy, soft stems that often branch near the base.
  • Thick, fleshy, broadly oval to round leaves with soft hairs.
  • Leaves in opposite pairs, with scalloped edges.
  • Leaves have a strong, pleasant aromatic smell when crushed.
  • Tiny pale purple or whitish flowers on a long spike at the top of the stem.

Traditional uses and importance

  • Recorded in herbal preparations traditionally associated with cough, cold and sore throat.
  • Traditionally associated with digestive complaints.
  • The leaves and leaf juice are mentioned in traditional literature.

Flowers and fruits

The plant bears very small flowers, each with both male and female parts, followed by small dry fruits. Flowering is uncommon in home gardens because the plant is mostly grown for its leaves. The thick, aromatic leaves are the best way to identify it.

Garden notes

Kapparavalli lives for many years and grows easily from stem cuttings. Its thick leaves, aromatic smell and simple flower spike make it useful for teaching plant identification, leaf structure and growing plants from cuttings.

Good to know

This plant is now often listed under the newer name Coleus amboinicus.

Safety note

Do not consume or prepare this plant as a home remedy without advice. Several closely related species look similar, so correct identification is essential.
