Cardamom

Elettaria cardamomum (L.) Maton
එනසාල්
  • Local nameEnasal (එනසාල්)
  • ScientificElettaria cardamomum (L.) Maton
  • FamilyZingiberaceae (Ginger family)
  • Plant habitPerennial herb

About this plant

Cardamom is a perennial aromatic herb native to the Western Ghats of southern India and one of the world's most valuable spices. It belongs to the ginger family and is grown for its aromatic seeds. In Sri Lanka it is mainly grown in the wet and intermediate zones.

How to identify it

  • Usually grows about 2 to 4 m tall.
  • Leafy shoots grow from underground rhizomes.
  • Upright stems covered with long leaf sheaths.
  • Long, lance-shaped leaves with smooth edges and a pointed tip.
  • Small, pale greenish-white flowers with violet markings.
  • Small oval capsules containing several aromatic seeds.

Traditional uses and importance

  • The dried capsules flavour curries, sweets, desserts and baked goods.
  • Commonly used to flavour tea, coffee and other drinks.
  • The seeds are used in spice mixtures and traditional dishes.
  • Cardamom oil is used in the food, fragrance and cosmetic industries.
  • In traditional medicine, it has been used for digestive complaints.
  • An important export spice for Sri Lanka.

Flowers and fruits

Flowers appear on separate shoots that grow out from the base of the plant, often lying close to the ground. They develop into small capsules that are green when young and turn yellowish or pale brown as they mature. Each capsule holds several small dark seeds, and the dried capsules and seeds are the spice we call cardamom.

Garden notes

Cardamom grows well in warm, humid climates with partial shade and well-drained, fertile soil rich in organic matter. It is usually propagated from rhizomes or seeds. It is a good plant for studying underground stems, flower structures and spice crops.

Safety note

Cardamom is generally used as a spice in normal cooking amounts. Avoid using concentrated preparations as home remedies without professional advice.
