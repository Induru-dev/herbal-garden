Aromatic Grass

Vetiver

Chrysopogon zizanioides (L.) Roberty
සැවැන්දරා
  • Local nameSavandara (සැවැන්දරා)
  • ScientificChrysopogon zizanioides (L.) Roberty
  • FamilyPoaceae (Grass family)
  • Plant habitTall, tufted perennial grass

About this plant

Vetiver is a tall, tufted perennial grass famous for its deep, fragrant roots. It is valued for its aromatic root oil, for traditional cooling drinks, and for controlling soil erosion on slopes.

How to identify it

  • A dense, upright clump of stiff, narrow leaves that can reach 1 to 2 m tall.
  • Leaves are long, flat or slightly folded, with a rough edge.
  • Its most distinctive feature is a thick, fibrous root system that grows straight down to a great depth.
  • The roots have a sweet, earthy fragrance when dug up or crushed.

Traditional uses and importance

  • The roots are soaked in water to make a cooling drink and are used in traditional practices in Sri Lanka and India.
  • Vetiver oil, distilled from the roots, is used in perfumery and aromatherapy.
  • The roots are woven into mats and screens that scent a room.
  • The leaves are used for thatching and mulch.

Garden notes

Vetiver's deep roots bind soil, so it is planted in close rows along slopes to reduce erosion and landslides. It grows well in full sun and tolerates poor soil, drought and flooding. It is propagated by splitting clumps into slips, and because it rarely sets viable seed it does not spread aggressively. It is a good plant for teaching visitors about soil conservation.

Safety note

The roots are used in drinks in small traditional amounts. Vetiver oil is for external or fragrance use only and should be diluted before skin contact. Anyone who is pregnant or on medication should consult a health professional before using it medicinally.
