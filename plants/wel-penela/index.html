Medicinal Climber

Wel Penela (Balloon Vine)

Cardiospermum halicacabum L.
වැල් පෙනෙල
  • Local nameWel Penela (වැල් පෙනෙල)
  • ScientificCardiospermum halicacabum L.
  • FamilySapindaceae (Soapberry family)
  • Plant habitSlender climbing vine, annual or short-lived perennial

About this plant

Wel Penela (balloon vine) is a slender climbing medicinal vine found throughout Sri Lanka in home gardens, hedges and waste ground. It is easy to recognise by its papery, balloon-like fruits. Its scientific name means "heart seed", because each black seed carries a small white heart-shaped mark.

How to identify it

  • A slender, climbing vine that scrambles over hedges and fences.
  • Thin, grooved green stems.
  • Compound leaves divided into smaller leaflets with deeply toothed edges.
  • Curly tendrils near the flower clusters help the plant climb.
  • Small white flowers with four petals.
  • Inflated, papery, three-sided fruits that look like small green lanterns or balloons.
  • Round black seeds, each with a white heart-shaped spot.

Traditional uses and importance

  • The leaves are cooked or eaten as a leafy vegetable and used in herbal porridge (kola kenda) in Sri Lanka.
  • In Ayurveda and Sri Lankan traditional medicine, the plant is traditionally used for joint pain and rheumatism.
  • Leaf pastes are traditionally applied externally for swelling and skin complaints.
  • Used in some traditional herbal hair oils.

Flowers and fruits

Small white flowers grow in loose clusters on long stalks, together with a pair of curly tendrils. After pollination each flower forms a light, inflated capsule that is green at first and turns pale brown and papery as it dries. Inside are three round black seeds, each marked with a white heart.

Garden notes

Wel Penela grows easily in sunny or partly shaded places with moist, well-drained soil. It grows quickly from seed and needs a fence or trellis to climb. It can self-seed and spread, so trim it back after fruiting. Its balloon-like fruits and heart-marked seeds make it a fun plant for teaching visitors about tendrils, climbing plants and seed dispersal.

Safety note

Traditional uses do not prove that a plant can treat disease. Do not prepare medicinal remedies from this plant without correct identification and advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
