Monarakudumbiya

About this plant

Monarakudumbiya is a small, widespread herb found in waste land and gardens throughout Sri Lanka. Its flower head looks like a peacock's crest, which gives it its local name ("Monara" means peacock and "kudumbiya" means crest or top-knot).

How to identify it

  • Usually grows about 15 to 30 cm tall, sometimes taller in good conditions.
  • A slender, slightly hairy stem.
  • Simple leaves with shallowly toothed or smooth margins.
  • Small purple, pink or lilac flower heads clustered at the top, like a peacock's crest.

Traditional uses and importance

  • Liver health: famously used in herbal porridges (kola kenda) traditionally taken for jaundice and to support the liver and digestion.
  • Blood: traditionally believed to purify the blood and boost immunity.
  • Fever and worms: the whole plant or its juice is recorded in traditional remedies for fevers, skin irritations and worm infections.

Garden notes

Monarakudumbiya grows easily in sunny, open places and often comes up by itself from wind-blown seeds.

Good to know

This plant is now often listed under the newer name Cyanthillium cinereum.

Safety note

Although it is eaten as a traditional herbal porridge, anyone wanting to use it as a treatment for a medical condition should consult a qualified herbalist or healthcare professional.
