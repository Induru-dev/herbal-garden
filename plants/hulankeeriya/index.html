Arrowroot

Maranta arundinacea L.
හුලංකීරිය
  • Local nameHulankeeriya (හුලංකීරිය)
  • ScientificMaranta arundinacea L.
  • FamilyMarantaceae (Arrowroot family)
  • Plant habitPerennial herb with starchy rhizomes

About this plant

Hulankeeriya (arrowroot) is a traditional food plant that grows readily in the wet zone of Sri Lanka. Its white underground rhizomes are rich in starch and are used to make arrowroot flour.

How to identify it

  • Grows as a leafy clump in the wet zone.
  • Dark green, elongated, narrow leaves.
  • White underground rhizomes with ring-like ridges, slim at the base and thicker towards a pointed tip.
  • Rhizomes are ready to harvest about 8 to 10 months after planting.

Traditional uses and importance

  • The rhizomes are processed into arrowroot flour.
  • The flour is used to make porridge and other foods, and is traditionally given to infants as an easy-to-digest food.
  • Used as a thickening agent in cooking.
  • A good source of carbohydrate, with about 2% protein and small amounts of vitamins and calcium.
  • Traditionally associated with relieving indigestion.

Flowers and fruits

The flowers are small, white and tubular, and both male and female parts occur in the same flower. They grow in pairs on branched flower stalks. Some botanical records report flowering from June to August. The underground rhizomes are the edible part of the plant.

Garden notes

Hulankeeriya grows readily in the warm, moist conditions of the wet zone. It is grown from pieces of rhizome and harvested after about 8 to 10 months.

Safety note

Arrowroot flour is a common food. Traditional health uses should not replace medical advice.
