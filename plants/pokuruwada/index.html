Coral Hibiscus

Hibiscus schizopetalus (Dyer) Hook.f.

About this plant

Pokuruwada (coral hibiscus) is an attractive evergreen shrub widely grown in Sri Lanka and other tropical regions. It is easy to recognise by its graceful, arching branches and unusual hanging flowers.

How to identify it

  • Growth: a fast-growing shrub, usually 2 to 3 m tall, with slender, drooping branches.
  • Leaves: simple, alternate and oval, with toothed edges and a glossy green surface.
  • Flowers: red or pinkish-red flowers hanging on long, slender stalks, with deeply fringed petals that curve backward around a long, hanging column of stamens.

Traditional uses and importance

  • Hibiscus flowers and leaves are traditionally used in soothing herbal infusions and cooling preparations.
  • Traditionally used in natural hair-care remedies.
  • Widely grown as an ornamental plant for its striking flowers.

Flowers and fruits

The plant flowers throughout the year, attracting pollinators such as sunbirds and butterflies. After pollination it can form a dry, five-part capsule with small seeds, but fruit is rare on garden plants, which are mostly grown from stem cuttings.

Garden notes

Pokuruwada flowers all year round and has a striking flower structure, making it a great example for teaching about flower adaptations, pollination and growing plants from cuttings.

Safety note

These records reflect traditional knowledge, not proven medical treatments. Do not prepare any part of this plant as a home remedy without proper guidance.
