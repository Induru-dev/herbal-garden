Rasakinda

Tinospora cordifolia (Willd.) Hook.f. & Thomson
රසකිඳ
  • Local nameRasakinda (රසකිඳ)
  • ScientificTinospora cordifolia (Willd.) Hook.f. & Thomson
  • FamilyMenispermaceae (Moonseed family)
  • Plant habitLarge, deciduous perennial climbing shrub

About this plant

Rasakinda (heart-leaved moonseed, known as guduchi or giloy in India) is a large climbing plant found in Sri Lanka, India and Myanmar. It is one of the most important plants in Ayurveda, where its stem is the main part used. It is easy to spot climbing over fences and trees, with heart-shaped leaves and long, thin aerial roots hanging down.

How to identify it

  • A large, climbing shrub that twines over trees, fences and walls.
  • Heart-shaped, smooth, green leaves on long stalks.
  • Succulent stems with a corky, greyish-brown bark covered in small raised spots.
  • Long, thread-like aerial roots that hang down from the stems.
  • The stem tastes very bitter.

Traditional uses and importance

  • The stem is the main part used in Ayurvedic and Sri Lankan traditional medicine.
  • Traditionally used in preparations for fever.
  • Traditionally valued as a general tonic and to support the body's natural defences.
  • Traditionally associated with digestive complaints and blood sugar management.
  • The bitter stem starch, known as guduchi satva, is used in traditional preparations.

Flowers and fruits

Small, greenish-yellow flowers appear on long, slender stalks, often when the plant has lost its leaves. Male and female flowers grow on separate plants. Female flowers develop into small, round, fleshy fruits in clusters, which turn bright red when ripe.

Garden notes

Rasakinda grows well in warm tropical climates and is very hardy. It needs a fence, trellis or tree to climb on. It is easily grown from stem cuttings, which root quickly even from thick pieces of stem. It is a good plant for teaching visitors about climbing plants, aerial roots and Ayurvedic medicine.

Safety note

Traditional uses do not prove that a plant can treat disease. Concentrated preparations have been linked to liver problems in some people, and it may lower blood sugar. Do not use it as a home remedy without advice from a qualified healthcare professional.
